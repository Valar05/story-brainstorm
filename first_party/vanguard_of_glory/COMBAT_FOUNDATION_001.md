## Combat thesis

Vanguard's best source combat is not defined by named techniques.

It is defined by **readable force management under material constraints**.

The recurring grammar is:

1. read what is generating force;
2. identify what part of the opponent or environment has committed;
3. choose whether to redirect, remove support, reinforce, bind, or overpower;
4. spend Aether/essence only where the force path requires it;
5. accept that armor can save an error but cannot make geometry correct;
6. carry the resulting position into the next exchange.

## Layowich guild-pit lesson

The large duelist spends Aether throughout his body and attacks relentlessly.

The smaller fighter does not answer strength with strength.

He:
- conserves energy;
- sidesteps;
- deflects;
- waits for impatience;
- alters the ground where the attacker is about to plant;
- forces a bad step;
- exits the overhead line;
- sweeps the compromised support;
- adds a shove;
- converts knockdown into a grapple;
- ends when the opponent yields.

This establishes a core Vanguard-of-Glory law:

**The strongest attack can create the best opening when its support and recovery are made unreliable.**

The fight is legible because each action changes the physical state required by the next.

## Alpha-wolf exchange

The alpha is armored and faster than its silhouette suggests.

Vanguard:
- chooses a murder-strike orientation because the armor makes edge-first cutting unreliable;
- accelerates into the engagement with battery-supported movement;
- commits the hilt downward;
- the wolf catches the weapon in its jaws;
- Vanguard overcharges Mass;
- the weapon becomes too heavy for the wolf to hold;
- both wolf and weapon crash to the ground;
- the jaw armor cracks;
- Vanguard attempts another Mass-heavy overhead smash;
- the wolf evades;
- she discovers the cost of her own solution: Mass makes the committed weapon hard to redirect;
- she pulls Mass back out to regain handling speed;
- the weapon is still out of position;
- the alpha re-enters with body and foreclaws;
- Vanguard reinforces armor but not her own body mass;
- armor prevents catastrophic injury, but momentum still drives her to the ground;
- the weapon is lost;
- she recognizes the mistake after contact;
- she uses a battery-heavy uppercut against the already cracked skull plate;
- she draws the dagger;
- infuses Sharpness;
- places the thrust into the armor gap;
- converts a localized structural failure into lethal penetration;
- retrieves the sword;
- continues smashing until the head is physically destroyed.

This is unusually rich combat because **every solution leaves a residue**.

Mass solves the jaw bind but creates handling debt.
Armor solves penetration but not knockdown.
The first impact creates the crack that makes the later uppercut and dagger thrust meaningful.
Weapon loss changes the available move set.
The kill comes from exploiting accumulated structural damage, not from an unrelated finishing spell.

## Blaze contrast

Blaze is presented as visibly smoother.

His motion:
- has purpose at every step;
- uses one enemy to influence another;
- converts an impaled body into a moving obstacle/weapon;
- uses reach to control multiple attackers;
- uses terrain/fire to partition the encounter.

Vanguard explicitly feels clumsy by comparison.

This matters.

The source does not protect Vanguard's ego by making her automatically the cleanest fighter in every room.

Her growth can therefore be measured against visible examples of better economy.

## RPG action economy

The RPG draft contains a useful abstract skeleton:

- player begins with a small action budget;
- favorable/unfavorable initiation changes starting economy;
- defend preserves action economy while reducing damage;
- Spare gains future action;
- Slaughter restores health;
- attacks trade damage, control, cooldown, and action cost.

This is valuable because it encodes the same prose principle in game form:

**combat is not only damage; it is the management of future options.**

Kick removes an enemy action.
Break Face increases future vulnerability.
Riposte converts defense into a return strike.
Divine Blade spends immediate actions to create a temporary offensive state.

## Transferable combat rules

### Rule 1 — Armor is margin, not strategy
Armor permits survival through an error. It does not make the error correct.

### Rule 2 — Support is part of every attack
Foot placement, stance, mass, and committed direction are attack components. Attack the support and the attack changes.

### Rule 3 — Power has handling debt
Increasing Mass or commitment improves consequence while reducing the ability to redirect.

### Rule 4 — Damage should accumulate structurally
A cracked plate, lost grip, bad foot, displaced weapon, or exposed gap should change later options.

### Rule 5 — Spend only where force travels
Reinforcement should be local and causal whenever possible.

### Rule 6 — Better fighters look economical
Skill should often appear as fewer wasted movements, less unnecessary Aether, and cleaner conversion of enemy motion.

### Rule 7 — A mistake should create the next problem
Do not erase errors between beats. Let them alter range, weapon ownership, footing, damage, and available responses.

### Rule 8 — Finishers emerge from state
The dagger kill works because previous exchanges created a specific broken armor gap.

### Rule 9 — Violence ends when the actual threat state changes
The guild duel stops on yield. The alpha fight continues until the dangerous body is physically incapable of returning.

### Rule 10 — Combat and material world are continuous
Money, crystals, armor, food, rest, repair, travel, and advancement all determine what violence costs before and after the exchange.

## Why this matters to current Vanguard

These are foundation mechanics, not merely aesthetic inspiration.

Current Vanguard's kinetic compression, shoulder pressure, scattergun/bayonet control, and grounded action grammar should inherit the **causal discipline** of this combat even when the specific Aether system and source events do not cross continuity.
