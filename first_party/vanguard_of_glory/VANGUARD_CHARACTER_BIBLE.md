# Vanguard of Glory — Vanguard Character Bible

## Core construction

Vanguard is dangerous because she notices material state.

She pays attention to:
- where force is coming from
- what the ground will do
- whether her weapon is overcommitted
- whether armor is actually carrying load
- who controls local trade
- who is watching her money
- whether a bath, meal, stable, room, or armor-cleaning arrangement is safe
- how much Aether is present in the environment
- what a resource costs in future advancement
- whether a person is bluffing, haggling, sizing her up, or genuinely competent

Her competence is not presented as generalized "badassery." It is distributed across dozens of small practical judgments.

## Young Vanguard

The useful origin-era traits are:

- capable, not finished
- materially curious
- willing to comply with local rules until they become predatory
- dry sense of humor
- enjoys good food without pretending not to
- suspicious of guild capture and local monopolies
- does not automatically seek social dominance
- becomes aggressive quickly when a threat becomes explicit
- learns by watching better fighters
- notices her own mistakes after consequences make them undeniable
- does not confuse armor with invulnerability
- carries the emotional reality of fatigue, baths, clean clothes, gear maintenance, hunger, money, and travel time

## Voice

Her dialogue tends to be:
- short
- practical
- mildly sardonic
- rarely ceremonial
- responsive to the actual problem in front of her

Example construction:
Harrick theatrically complains that she is ruining him.
Vanguard does not out-theatricalize him. She answers with one dry practical jab and keeps bargaining.

The humor works because it rides on real material stakes.

## Social posture

Vanguard does not enter Layowich demanding recognition.

She:
1. hears the guard's classification
2. complies with registration
3. protects her valuables
4. pays stable fees
5. buys food
6. asks a vendor for local intelligence
7. books lodging
8. bathes
9. cleans gear
10. makes potions
11. sells crystals
12. responds only when local guild predation reaches her directly

This is extremely important.

She is not antisocial.
She is not anti-institution by default.
She becomes hostile when an institution stops functioning as structure and starts functioning as extraction.

## Learning posture

The public duel is a miniature curriculum.

She watches:
- the stronger fighter spend too much
- the smaller fighter conserve
- a foot-placement trap
- a sweep timed to imbalance
- a transition to grappling
- victory through skill economy

Later, in her own fight with the alpha, she makes the opposite error:
- overcommits Mass
- cannot redirect
- loses weapon position
- gets bowled over

The character is therefore internally consistent:
she values economy before she fully embodies it.

## Origin-story transfer

For the Inner Heart game, preserve this younger state:
- she can be impressed
- she can be outclassed technically
- she can learn from local fighters and nonhuman cultures
- she can make expensive mistakes
- she can misread a system
- she can enjoy a meal
- she can be annoyed by bureaucracy
- she can bargain
- she can owe people
- she can become locally known before becoming mythic

Do not write her as the fully formed later legend arriving from page one.
